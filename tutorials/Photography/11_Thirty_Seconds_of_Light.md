# 🎞️ Mini-Project 2: Photo-Film and Series

**Time:** 5 hours total — 4 hours of production and 1 hour of presentation and screening

**Goal:** Create a coherent series of five black-and-white photographs, export them as print-ready TIFF files, and assemble the same images into a 15-second photo-film using sequencing, duration, rhythm, and sound.

You will work with a partner for technical support, location safety, camera assistance, and feedback. Each participant must photograph, edit, print, assemble, and present their own project.

## Final outcomes

### Printed photography series

```text
5 black-and-white photographs
16:9 landscape format
3200 × 1800 pixels
TIFF
300 pixels/inch
sRGB
```

### Photo-film

```text
Exactly 15 seconds
1920 × 1080
30 fps
H.264 MP4
5 photographs
Black and white
```

## Production schedule

| Stage | Suggested time |
|---|---:|
| Review examples | 15 minutes |
| Plan the series and photo-film | 15 minutes |
| Prepare the camera and location | 10 minutes |
| Photograph the sequence | 50 minutes |
| Transfer and select photographs | 15 minutes |
| Edit and export the TIFF series | 70 minutes |
| Assemble and export the photo-film | 55 minutes |
| Complete the final check | 10 minutes |
| Presentation and screening | 60 minutes |

## Project concept

Create an **observational micro-narrative** using five photographs made in one general location.

An observational micro-narrative may focus on:

- A person moving through a place
- A repeated action or gesture
- A gradual reveal of a subject
- A relationship between a person, object, and location
- A change in distance, light, focus, or perspective
- An overlooked event or detail
- A particular atmosphere
- A visual pattern that develops across several photographs

The idea must be communicated through the photographs rather than through dialogue or explanatory text.

## One project, two outcomes

### Printed series

The five photographs will be printed and displayed in a specific order. Meaning develops through image selection, consistent format, repetition, variation, and movement from one print to the next.

### Photo-film

The same five photographs will be arranged in Premiere Pro. Meaning develops through image order, duration, rhythm, pacing, cuts, sound or intentional silence, titles, and credits.

> Do not treat the photo-film as a slideshow of unrelated images. Both outcomes must communicate one coherent idea.

## Project requirements

- Create exactly **five final photographs**
- Photograph in colour and convert the final images to black and white
- Use landscape orientation
- Use a `16:9` aspect ratio
- Create all photographs during the activity
- Work within one general location
- Use at least three different shot sizes
- Use one or two compositional frameworks
- Make intentional decisions about depth of field
- Do not use dialogue
- Do not use video footage
- Do not animate the photographs
- Do not use generated imagery

## Select one location and idea

Choose a location that can be reached quickly and photographed safely.

Your project may focus on:

- A person completing one simple action
- An object being moved, used, opened, closed, or transformed
- A path through a location
- A sequence of shadows or reflections
- A relationship between architecture and the body
- A change from a wider view to a small detail
- A repeated shape, texture, colour, or gesture
- An atmosphere created by light, weather, or movement

## Plan the five photographs

Use this structure as a starting point:

1. **Establish:** Introduce the location, subject, or situation.
2. **Approach:** Move closer or reveal an important relationship.
3. **Develop:** Show an action, gesture, detail, or visual change.
4. **Shift:** Introduce contrast, interruption, movement, or a new perspective.
5. **Conclude:** End with resolution, ambiguity, or a final observation.

The sequence does not need to tell a literal story. It must create a clear visual progression.

## Use shot sizes intentionally

Include at least three:

- Long shot
- Full shot
- Medium full shot
- Medium shot
- Medium close-up
- Close-up
- Extreme close-up

Check the 📐 [Photography Composition Reference Guide](00_Composition_Reference.md){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}.

## Use composition and depth

Use one or two compositional frameworks consistently:

- Rule of thirds
- Leading lines
- Centred and symmetrical composition
- Golden ratio
- Repetition
- Framing within the frame
- Foreground, middle-ground, and background

Review:

- 🌗 [Exposure Triangle Reference Guide](00_Exposure_Triangle.md){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}

## Establish the starting exposure

Begin with:

```text
Aperture: f/8
Shutter speed: 1/125
ISO: 400
```

Take a test photograph and review focus, histogram, highlight detail, shadow detail, movement, and depth of field.

Adjust the settings according to the scene:

- Use a faster shutter speed when movement must appear sharp.
- Use a slower shutter speed when motion blur is intentional.
- Use a lower f-number for a shallower depth of field.
- Use a higher f-number when more of the scene must remain sharp.
- Increase ISO only when the required aperture and shutter speed produce an unusably dark photograph.

> The exposure settings may change between compositions. The five final photographs must still have a consistent overall brightness, contrast, and visual treatment.

You have approximately **50 minutes** to photograph.

## Working in pairs

Work with a partner for technical support, tripod assistance, feedback, continuity, participation as a subject, and safety. Each participant must create a separate project.

Do not photograph identifiable strangers without permission. Do not block entrances, hallways, classrooms, paths, or accessibility routes.

## Photographing process

For each planned photograph:

1. Establish the shot size and composition.
2. Select the focal length.
3. Move the camera to the required distance.
4. Adjust the aperture, shutter speed, and ISO.
5. Refocus manually.
6. Check the edges of the frame.
7. Take a test photograph.
8. Review the histogram and magnify the focus point.
9. Make corrections.
10. Photograph at least three usable variations.

Aim to create approximately **15–20 photographs** in total. You will select five.

## Required variation

Across the complete sequence:

- Use at least three shot sizes
- Include at least one wider environmental view
- Include at least one close-up or extreme close-up
- Change camera distance or viewpoint
- Use one or two compositional frameworks
- Make at least one intentional depth-of-field decision
- Maintain the same `16:9` landscape format
- Maintain a recognizable visual relationship between the photographs

## Develop the five RAW files

Open the five selected `.CR2` files in Adobe Camera Raw and arrange them in sequence.

For all five photographs:

- Convert the image to black and white
- Correct exposure
- Adjust contrast
- Preserve important highlight detail
- Preserve required shadow detail
- Refine whites and blacks
- Adjust texture or clarity carefully
- Avoid excessive sharpening
- Avoid heavy noise reduction that removes important detail

## Create one visual treatment

The photographs should share:

- A consistent black-and-white treatment
- Related brightness and contrast
- Similar highlight and shadow detail
- A consistent crop and format
- A related level of texture and clarity
- A coherent visual atmosphere

Consistency does not require identical slider values. Each photograph may need small corrections.

## Restrictions

Do not:

- Remove or add objects
- Retouch extensively
- Apply decorative filters
- Add textures
- Add generated imagery
- Add typography to the printed photographs
- Use different aspect ratios

## Open in Photoshop

For each photograph:

1. Confirm the crop is `16:9`.
2. Set the dimensions to `3200 × 1800 pixels`.
3. Set the resolution to `300 pixels/inch`.
4. Confirm the orientation is landscape.
5. Convert to `sRGB`.
6. Compare all five images together.
7. Make final tonal corrections when necessary.

## Save editable PSD files

Save in the **PSD** folder:

```text
Name-Lastname-Series-01.psd
Name-Lastname-Series-02.psd
Name-Lastname-Series-03.psd
Name-Lastname-Series-04.psd
Name-Lastname-Series-05.psd
```

Keep layers enabled.

## Export print-ready TIFF files

For each photograph:

1. Select **File → Save a Copy** or **File → Save As**.
2. Select **TIFF (`.tif`)**.
3. Use:
   - **Dimensions:** `3200 × 1800 pixels`
   - **Resolution:** `300 pixels/inch`
   - **Bit depth:** `8 Bits/Channel`
   - **Colour profile:** `sRGB`
   - **Embed Colour Profile:** Enabled
   - **Image compression:** LZW or ZIP
   - **Layers:** Off for the print copy
4. Save in the **TIFF-Print** folder.

```text
Name-Lastname-Series-01.tif
Name-Lastname-Series-02.tif
Name-Lastname-Series-03.tif
Name-Lastname-Series-04.tif
Name-Lastname-Series-05.tif
```

## Print the series

Follow the paper, printer, and print-dialogue settings provided by the instructor.

- Print one photograph per sheet
- Maintain the `16:9` aspect ratio
- Do not stretch or distort the image
- Centre the photograph on the page
- Use the same paper and print settings for all five photographs
- Keep the five prints in sequence

> The TIFF files are the print masters. Do not print from screenshots or compressed preview files.

<div class="section-content" markdown="1">

## Create the Premiere Pro project

Save in the **Premiere** folder:

```text
Name-Lastname-Photo-Film.prproj
```

## Create the sequence

Use:

- **Resolution:** `1920 × 1080`
- **Aspect ratio:** `16:9`
- **Frame rate:** `30 fps`
- **Duration:** Exactly `15 seconds`

## Import the photographs

Import the five TIFF files from the **TIFF-Print** folder.

The photo-film must contain:

- Five still photographs
- A simple title
- Your name
- A final audio credit when sourced audio is used

## Arrange the sequence

Consider:

- Which photograph opens the work
- How long each image remains visible
- Where the pace becomes faster or slower
- Whether one image should create a longer pause
- How visual direction moves between frames
- How wide and close images relate
- How light, contrast, shapes, and subject placement connect
- How the ending differs from the beginning

The photographs do not need equal durations.

## Titles and credits

Include:

- Project title
- Your name
- Year
- Audio title and creator name when sourced audio is used

Example:

```text
Audio: “Track Title” — Creator Name
```

Keep typography simple and readable.

## Permitted transitions

You may use:

- Straight cuts
- Fade in
- Fade out
- Simple cross-dissolves

Do not use:

- Stylized transitions
- Motion presets
- Camera-movement effects
- Animated zooms or pans
- Filters
- Colour effects
- Additional visual effects

> The photographs must remain still. Create rhythm through image order and duration rather than animation.

## Sound

Use one approach:

- One instrumental or ambient music track
- One ambient sound recording
- Silence when it supports the concept

Do not use dialogue, lyrics, multiple unrelated audio tracks, or copyrighted commercial music without permission.

Royalty-free sources include:

- [Freesound](https://freesound.org){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}
- [Free Music Archive](https://freemusicarchive.org){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}
- [Pixabay Music](https://pixabay.com/music/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}
- [Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-stock-music/){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}
- [YouTube Audio Library](https://www.youtube.com/audiolibrary){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}

Before using sourced audio:

- Check the licence
- Record the track title
- Record the creator’s name
- Record the source
- Save the audio file in the **Audio** folder

For the on-screen audio credit, include only the audio title and creator name.

## Mix the audio

Check that:

- The audio does not clip
- The master output remains below `0 dB`
- The audio supports the photographs
- The audio begins and ends intentionally
- Fades do not cut off abruptly
- Silence is intentional when selected

## Export the photo-film

1. Select **File → Export → Media**.
2. Select **H.264**.
3. Use:
   - **Resolution:** `1920 × 1080`
   - **Frame rate:** `30 fps`
   - **Audio:** AAC
4. Confirm that the total duration is exactly `15 seconds`.
5. Export to the **Exported-Video** folder.

```text
Name-Lastname-Photo-Film.mp4
```

Watch the exported MP4 from beginning to end.

**Time:** 60 minutes

## Prepare the presentation

Arrange the five prints in the intended order. Open the exported MP4 and confirm that it is ready to play.

Each participant will have approximately **3–4 minutes**.

Present in this order:

```text
1. Printed photography series
2. Photo-film screening
3. Brief explanation
```

For both outcomes, briefly explain:

- What you observed
- What changed across the five photographs
- What remained visually consistent
- How sequencing affected the meaning

Finish by sharing:

- One result, problem, or discovery
- One compositional or editing decision that worked well
- One difference between viewing the photographs as prints and viewing them as a photo-film

## Listen and participate

While viewing the work of others:

1. Observe the printed sequence before the screening.
2. Watch the complete photo-film.
3. Identify one specific visual or time-based decision.
4. Contribute one respectful comment or question.

Useful comments include:

- “The close-up changes the meaning of the wider establishing image.”
- “The longer duration of the fourth image creates a strong pause.”
- “The black-and-white treatment remains consistent across the prints.”
- “The sound creates a different atmosphere than the printed series.”
- “The sequence moves clearly from the location to a small detail.”
- “The final photograph creates an effective unresolved ending.”

Avoid vague comments such as:

> “It looks good.”

Identify the specific photographic, sequencing, editing, or sound decision that worked.

## Reflection questions

1. How did the meaning change when the photographs were viewed as prints and as a timed sequence?
2. Which photograph was most important to the sequence, and why?
3. How did duration, pacing, or sound affect the photo-film?
4. Which visual decision helped the five photographs feel like one series?
5. What would you change if you had more production time?
