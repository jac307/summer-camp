## Before Turning On the Camera

1. Keep the power switch at `OFF`.
2. Confirm that the battery is charged and inserted.
3. Insert the SD card in the correct direction.
4. Confirm that the lens is securely attached.
5. Remove and safely store the lens cap.
6. Use the neck strap or hold the camera with both hands.
7. Turn the camera on only after the equipment is ready.

> Turn the camera off before removing the SD card, battery, or lens.

## Format the SD Card

Formatting prepares the card for a new shoot.

> **Warning:** Formatting permanently deletes every file on the card. Confirm that previous files have been transferred before continuing.

1. Turn on the camera.
2. Press `MENU`.
3. Open the yellow Setup menu.
4. Select **Format card**.
5. Select **OK**.
6. Press `SET`.
7. Wait until formatting is complete.

Do not turn off the camera or remove the SD card during formatting.

## Image Quality: RAW + JPEG

Select **RAW + Large/Fine JPEG**.

| File | Use |
|---|---|
| **RAW / `.CR2`** | Retains more image information for editing and correction. |
| **JPEG / `.JPG`** | Camera-processed file for quick review, contact sheets, technical GIFs, and sharing. |

Keep both files when transferring photographs.

## Exposure in Aperture Priority Mode

**Exposure** describes how light or dark a photograph appears.

![Underexposed, balanced, and overexposed versions of the same scene](imgs/exposure-comparison.webp){: .tutorial-image }

| Exposure | Appearance |
|---|---|
| **Underexposed** | Too dark; important shadow detail may be difficult to see. |
| **Balanced** | Useful detail remains visible in bright and dark areas. |
| **Overexposed** | Too bright; important highlight detail may become white. |

In `Av` mode:

- You select **aperture**
- You select **ISO**
- The camera selects **shutter speed**

Watch the shutter speed whenever aperture or ISO changes.

> When photographing handheld, a shutter speed slower than approximately `1/60` second may create accidental camera shake. Use a wider aperture, increase ISO, or use a tripod.

---

## Aperture

Aperture is the adjustable opening inside the lens. It controls light and influences depth of field.

![Aperture scale and f-stop values](imgs/aperture-graph.png){: .tutorial-image }

| Aperture | Lens opening | Visual effect |
|---|---|---|
| `f/3.5`–`f/5.6` | Wider | More light and a shallower depth of field |
| `f/8` | Medium | Balanced starting point for many subjects |
| `f/11`–`f/16` | Narrower | Less light and a deeper depth of field |

Remember:

- **Lower f-number = wider aperture**
- **Higher f-number = narrower aperture**

With the 18–55 mm kit lens:

- At approximately `18 mm`, the widest available aperture may be `f/3.5`
- At approximately `55 mm`, the widest available aperture may be `f/5.6`

### To change aperture

1. Set the Mode Dial to `Av`.
2. Turn the Main Control Dial.
3. Watch the f-number and shutter-speed values change.

## ISO

ISO affects the amplification of the light recorded by the sensor.

| ISO | Suggested use | Image quality |
|---:|---|---|
| `100` | Bright outdoor light or tripod work | Cleanest image |
| `200–400` | Cloudy conditions or open shade | Low visible noise |
| `400–800` | Bright indoor locations | Moderate noise may appear |
| `800–1600` | Darker indoor locations | More visible noise |
| `3200–6400` | Very low light when a faster shutter is necessary | Stronger noise and reduced fine detail |

### To change ISO

1. Press the `ISO` button.
2. Turn the Main Control Dial.
3. Press `SET`.

Use the lowest ISO that provides a usable shutter speed.

## White Balance

White balance controls how the camera interprets the colour of the light.

| Preset | Typical use |
|---|---|
| **AWB / Auto** | Quick general photography; colour may change between photographs |
| **Daylight** | Direct sunlight and the default for camp comparisons |
| **Shade** | Open shade; adds warmth |
| **Cloudy** | Overcast or late-day light; adds some warmth |
| **Tungsten** | Warm household bulbs; reduces orange colour casts |
| **White Fluorescent** | Fluorescent lighting; reduces cool or green colour casts |
| **Flash** | Photographs illuminated by flash |
| **Custom** | Uses a neutral reference; introduced in a later activity |

### To change white balance

1. Press `WB`.
2. Use the navigation buttons to select a preset.
3. Press `SET`.

Return the camera to **Daylight** after a white-balance comparison.

## Focus, Zoom, and Image Stabilization

### General photography

1. Set the lens to `AF`.
2. Place the focus point over the main subject.
3. Press the shutter button halfway.
4. Confirm focus.
5. Press fully to take the photograph.

### Controlled comparisons

1. Use autofocus to focus once.
2. Confirm that the subject is sharp.
3. Move the lens switch from `AF` to `MF`.
4. Do not turn the focus ring.
5. Keep focus unchanged while comparing aperture, ISO, or white balance.

### Image stabilization

| Camera support | Stabilizer |
|---|---|
| Handheld | `ON` |
| Tripod | `OFF` |

## Zoom and Focal Length

The 18–55 mm lens changes the field of view.

| Focal length | Effect |
|---|---|
| Approximately `18 mm` | Wider view; shows more of the location |
| Approximately `35 mm` | Medium field of view |
| Approximately `55 mm` | Narrower view; isolates the subject more strongly |

Turn the **zoom ring**, not the focus ring, to change focal length.

For controlled comparisons, select one focal length and do not change it.

## Review Every Important Photograph

1. Press the Playback button.
2. Use the navigation buttons to locate the image.
3. Check the complete composition.
4. Press Zoom in.
5. Inspect the main subject for sharpness.
6. Check bright and dark areas for lost detail.
7. Retake the photograph when necessary.

Do not rely only on the small full-image preview when checking focus.

## Exposure Compensation and AEB

### Exposure compensation

Use exposure compensation when the camera’s recommended exposure appears too dark or too bright.

- Negative values make the photograph darker
- Positive values make the photograph brighter
- Return the setting to `0` after use

### Auto Exposure Bracketing

AEB creates:

- One darker photograph
- One photograph at the camera’s recommended exposure
- One brighter photograph

To activate AEB:

1. Press `Q`.
2. Select the Exposure Compensation/AEB scale.
3. Turn the Main Control Dial until three markers appear.
4. Begin with approximately `-1`, `0`, and `+1`.

> Turn AEB off after the comparison. Bring the three markers together at `0`.

## Common Problems

| Problem | Check |
|---|---|
| **RAW is unavailable** | Set the Mode Dial to `Av`. |
| **Grid or 16:9 frame is not visible** | Activate Live View. |
| **Photographs have different colours** | Use a fixed white balance such as Daylight instead of AWB. |
| **Background is not blurred** | Use a lower f-number, zoom toward 55 mm, move closer to the subject, or increase the distance between subject and background. |
| **Photograph is blurry** | Confirm focus, check shutter speed, increase ISO, use a wider aperture, or use a tripod. |
| **Photographs are unexpectedly darker and brighter** | Turn Auto Exposure Bracketing off. |
| **Camera creates two files per photograph** | This is correct when image quality is set to RAW + JPEG. |
| **Camera does not recognize the SD card** | Turn off the camera, reinsert the card, and check that the card’s lock switch is not engaged. |
| **Camera will not turn on** | Check the battery charge and confirm that the battery compartment is closed. |

## Finish the Shoot

1. Review the photographs.
2. Turn off the camera.
3. Remove the SD card.
4. Copy the RAW and JPEG files to a clearly named folder.
5. Open several files to confirm the transfer.
6. Create a second copy on another drive or cloud-storage service.
7. Eject the SD card safely.
8. Return the camera settings to the required defaults.
9. Replace the lens cap.
10. Recharge the battery.
11. Return the complete camera kit.

> Do not format or erase the SD card until the files have been confirmed in at least two locations.
