# 🗣️ Technical Cards Exhibition and Group Review

**Time:** 45–60 minutes

**Goal:** Prepare and present your Shutter Speed and Focal Length and Distance technical cards, listen to the presentations of others, participate in the discussion, and complete a short reflection.

Use the **Technical Cards Exhibition Handout** throughout this activity.

Complete this activity after:

- 📸 [Manual Mode and Technical Card: Shutter Speed](08_Shutter_Priority_and_Movement.md){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}
- 🔭 [Focal Length, Camera Distance, Perspective, and Depth of Field](12_Focal_Length_and_Depth_of_Field.md){:target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}

1. Shutter Speed GIF

## Prepare the Shutter Speed presentation

Briefly explain:

- Which movement you photographed
- How the camera remained stationary on the tripod
- Which shutter-speed values were used
- Which aperture and ISO settings remained unchanged
- At which shutter speed motion blur became visible
- How the image brightness and histogram changed as the shutter speed became slower
- Which frame most clearly demonstrates frozen movement
- Which frame most clearly demonstrates motion blur

## Prepare the Focal Length and Distance presentation

Briefly explain:

- Which subject and background you selected
- Which focal lengths were used
- What remained unchanged in the **Same Camera Position** comparison
- How the field of view changed at `18 mm`, `50 mm`, and `135 mm`
- How you moved the camera to maintain similar subject framing
- How camera distance changed the apparent relationship between the subject and background
- How focal length and camera distance affected depth of field
- Which comparison most clearly demonstrates a change in perspective

## Finish the presentation by sharing

- One technical result, problem, or discovery
- One compositional decision that worked well
- One part of the comparison that was difficult to control
- One change you would make if you repeated either activity

> Write brief notes on the handout before presenting. Do not read every instruction from the tutorial. Focus on what you observed in your own photographs.

During the exhibition:

2. Observe the technical and compositional changes in both cards.
3. Complete the audience section of the handout.
4. Record at least one specific observation from another participant’s work.
5. Contribute at least one question or comment during the discussion.

## Observe the Shutter Speed GIFs

Look for:

- A stationary camera position
- Consistent framing and focus
- A repeated subject movement
- A clear progression from frozen movement to motion blur
- Accurate shutter-speed labels
- Changes in brightness caused by the slower shutter speeds
- A readable and consistently positioned text label

Useful comments include:

- “The movement begins to blur clearly at `1/60`.”
- “The camera framing remains consistent throughout the GIF.”
- “The background makes the subject’s movement easy to see.”
- “The difference between `1/500` and `1/30` is especially clear.”
- “The slower shutter speeds also produce a visible change in brightness.”
- “The repeated movement is consistent enough to compare the settings accurately.”

## Observe the Focal Length and Distance cards

Look for:

- Correct `18 mm`, `50 mm`, and `135 mm` labels
- A fixed camera position in the first comparison
- Similar subject size in the second comparison
- Changes in field of view
- Changes in camera distance
- Changes in the apparent size of the background
- Changes in the relationship between foreground, subject, and background
- Consistent exposure, focus, layout, and image dimensions

Useful comments include:

- “The fixed-position row clearly shows the field of view becoming narrower.”
- “The subject remains approximately the same size in the second row.”
- “The background appears larger behind the subject at `135 mm`.”
- “The closer `18 mm` position creates a stronger sense of distance between the subject and background.”
- “The card clearly separates changes in field of view from changes in perspective.”
- “The longer focal length and greater camera distance create the strongest subject separation.”

Avoid vague comments such as:

> “It looks good.”

Instead, identify the specific technical or compositional choice that worked.

<div class="section-content" markdown="1">

As a group, compare the technical cards and discuss:

## Shutter speed

- At which shutter speed did movement begin to appear blurred in different projects?
- How did the speed and direction of the subject affect the result?
- Which backgrounds made motion easiest to see?
- How did the histogram move as the shutter speed became slower?
- Which projects maintained the most consistent camera position, framing, and focus?
- How did keeping aperture and ISO fixed support the comparison?

## Focal length and camera distance

- What changed when focal length changed but the camera remained in the same position?
- What changed when the camera moved to maintain similar subject framing?
- Which cards most clearly demonstrate the difference between field of view and perspective?
- How did background distance affect the visibility of depth?
- How did `18 mm`, `50 mm`, and `135 mm` change the representation of space?
- Which combinations created a shallow or deep depth of field?

## Controlled comparison

Identify which variables remained unchanged in each activity.

| Activity | Setting that changed | Settings and conditions that remained unchanged |
|---|---|---|
| Shutter Speed | Shutter speed | Aperture, ISO, white balance, focal length, focus, framing, camera position, background, and lighting |
| Focal Length: Same Position | Focal length | Camera position, subject, background, exposure, white balance, tripod height, and camera angle |
| Focal Length: Similar Framing | Focal length and camera distance | Subject position, background, exposure, white balance, tripod height, and camera angle |

> A technical comparison is useful only when the variables are clearly controlled and the setting being examined produces an observable change.

## Reflection questions

1. At which shutter speed did movement begin to appear blurred in your GIF?

2. How did the image brightness and histogram change as the shutter speed became slower?

3. Which part of the Shutter Speed comparison was most difficult to keep consistent?

4. What changed when you photographed `18 mm`, `50 mm`, and `135 mm` from the same camera position?

5. What changed when you moved the camera to maintain similar subject framing?

6. How did camera distance affect the apparent relationship between the subject and background?

7. Which focal length and camera position created the strongest sense of depth or subject separation?

8. Which compositional decision worked best across the two activities?

9. What did you notice in another participant’s work that you may apply to your own photography?

10. What would you change if you repeated one of these technical comparisons?

11. Which concept do you now understand more clearly: shutter speed, field of view, perspective, camera distance, or depth of field? Explain why.

12. What will you pay closer attention to during the next photography activity?
