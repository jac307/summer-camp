# 🗣️ Group Review: Reading Camera Settings

**Time:** 45–60 minutes  

**Goal:** Present the Aperture, ISO, and White Balance technical cards; explain the camera settings used; identify what changed in each comparison; and discuss how technical choices affect photographic composition.

Complete this activity after:

- 📐 [Photography Composition Reference Guide](00_Composition_Reference.md)
- ⚙️ [Technical Cards: Aperture, ISO, and White Balance](03_Aperture_GIF.md)

---

## What You Will Present

Each participant will show:

1. **Aperture GIF**
2. **ISO Comparison Card**
3. **White Balance GIF**

Each presentation should explain:

- The camera settings used
- What remained unchanged
- What changed between frames
- What the comparison demonstrates
- How the technical setting affected the composition
- One problem, surprise, or discovery

---

## Presentation Format

Each participant has approximately **3–4 minutes**.

Use this order:

```text
1. Aperture GIF
2. ISO Comparison Card
3. White Balance GIF
4. Composition observations
5. One final finding
```

After each presentation, the group may offer:

- One technical observation
- One compositional observation
- One question

Keep comments specific, respectful, and connected to what is visible in the work.

Use this structure:

## Discuss the visual result

Address:

- Which part of the image remained sharp?
- Which background or foreground areas changed?
- At which aperture was the subject most clearly separated from the background?
- At which aperture did more of the scene become visible and sharp?
- Did the exposure remain visually consistent?
- Did a slower shutter speed introduce any blur?

## Connect aperture to composition

Discuss at least one:

- **Subject emphasis:** Did shallow depth of field isolate the subject?
- **Visual hierarchy:** Which element attracted attention first?
- **Foreground, middle ground, and background:** Which layers remained visible?
- **Shot size:** Did the framing make the depth-of-field change easy to see?
- **Background:** Did the background support or distract from the subject?
- **Mood:** Did shallow or deep focus change the atmosphere?

### Aperture presentation prompt

> The aperture setting that best supported my composition was __________ because ____________________________________________.

> As ISO increased, the shutter speed changed from __________________ to __________________.

> The first visible change in digital noise appeared at approximately ISO __________________.

> The clearest difference appeared in ________________________________.

## Discuss the visual result

Address:

- At which ISO did digital noise become noticeable?
- Was noise easier to see in shadows, flat areas, or detailed textures?
- Did fine detail become less clear at higher ISO values?
- Did the overall brightness remain similar?
- How did the camera’s shutter speed respond as ISO increased?
- Which ISO created the best balance between image quality and a usable shutter speed?

## Connect ISO to composition

ISO does not directly reorganize the frame, but it affects whether the composition can be recorded clearly.

Discuss at least one:

- Did a higher ISO help preserve a handheld composition by allowing a faster shutter speed?
- Did digital noise make dark areas visually heavier?
- Did noise reduce the visibility of textures or small details?
- Did the technical quality support or distract from the main subject?
- Would you accept more noise to preserve a specific moment or composition?

### ISO presentation prompt

> For this lighting situation, I would choose ISO __________ because ____________________________________________.

Address:

- Which preset made white or neutral objects appear most accurate?
- Which preset introduced a blue colour cast?
- Which preset introduced an orange or warm colour cast?
- Did AWB produce a neutral result?
- Which preset most closely represented the actual lighting?
- Which preset created the most interesting atmosphere?

## Connect white balance to composition

Colour changes can alter how the viewer reads the same arrangement.

Discuss at least one:

- Did a warm preset make one object stand out more strongly?
- Did a cool preset reduce or increase contrast between colours?
- Did the colour cast change the visual hierarchy?
- Did one preset make the image feel calm, tense, artificial, nostalgic, or clinical?
- Did the white balance support the intended subject and mood?

### White Balance presentation prompt

> The white-balance setting that best supported my composition was __________ because ____________________________________________.

## Identify the shot size

Choose the closest description:

- Long Shot
- Full Shot
- Medium Full Shot
- Medium Shot
- Medium Close-Up
- Close-Up
- Extreme Close-Up

## Identify the compositional framework

Choose one or more:

- Rule of Thirds
- Golden Ratio
- Leading Lines
- Centred and Symmetrical
- Another intentional arrangement

## Discuss the visual structure

Address:

- What is the main subject?
- What does the viewer notice first?
- Where is the subject positioned?
- Is the composition centred or asymmetrical?
- Are foreground, middle ground, and background visible?
- Are the frame edges clear of distractions?
- Is there useful negative space?
- Do the objects feel visually balanced?
- Does the background support the comparison?
- Was the same composition maintained across all frames?

> The viewer’s attention is directed by ________________________________.

Use observable language.

### Useful technical comments

- “The difference in background sharpness becomes clearest between `f/5.6` and `f/11`.”
- “Noise becomes more visible in the dark area at ISO 1600.”
- “The Tungsten preset creates the strongest blue colour cast.”
- “The shutter speed increased as the ISO increased.”
- “The framing remained consistent across every image.”

### Useful compositional comments

- “The centred arrangement makes the comparison easy to read.”
- “The diagonal line directs attention toward the main object.”
- “The shallow depth of field separates the foreground object.”
- “The bright background competes with the subject.”
- “The negative space helps the coloured object stand out.”

### Avoid vague comments

Instead of:

> “It looks good.”

Say:

> “The close framing and shallow depth of field make the textured object the clearest focal point.”

Instead of:

> “I do not like the background.”

Say:

> “The bright object at the right edge draws attention away from the main subject.”
